# Clinic hair removal orientation

SPDX-License-Identifier: CC-BY-4.0

<a id="nakabako.hair-removal.orientation"></a>
## Clinical fit and tradeoffs

Medical laser hair reduction can require repeated sessions and varies by person/body area; a hospital's session interval or count is its protocol, not a universal promise. Cosmetic removal should be compared as self-pay, as illustrated by the hospital service description;[^oim-selfpay] do not invent disease-based coverage exceptions. Ask the clinician about the actual method, expected endpoint and alternatives, including no procedure.[^hospital-laser]

Safety discussion must cover burns/skin injury, pain and pigmentation changes; historical consumer reports document harm even in medical settings. Their complaint counts cannot rank providers or estimate a patient's probability of harm.[^ncac-harm] Ask the clinician additionally about folliculitis, scarring, paradoxical increased hair, regrowth and management costs; verify each risk against the current procedure consent/device information rather than quote invented rates. Multidisciplinary guidance describes hair/skin-dependent results, including limited effect for white hair; never promise laser success for every hair type or exclude darker skin categorically.[^jda-cosmetic]

Do not match device/settings from a user's skin tone or hair description. Ask how the clinic assesses skin/hair, tanning, tattoos/lesions, prior reactions, medicines and any test-spot need; send detailed medical history directly to clinician. Confirm physician assessment and supervision, who performs treatment, qualifications, eye protection, cooling and complication response. Do not incorrectly require the physician to operate every laser personally or treat approval as proof of suitability. Nurses may assist under physician instructions; unqualified sales counselors cannot make individualized medical treatment decisions.[^mhlw-staff] Follicle-destroying laser/light is a medical act regardless of branding; salon IPL and clinic medical treatment must not be assumed equivalent.[^mhlw-laser] Seek another consultation if explanations are inadequate. Provider flags such as tanning, tattoos, skin problems and pregnancy/medicines require clinician review, not automated blanket exclusion or medication-stop advice.[^saiseikai]

## Affordability and advertising

Compare the **same area and exclusions** (face, VIO, neck or beard boundaries), method availability at that branch, sessions and completion horizon. Add consultation, shaving, anesthesia, medicine, aftercare, extra sessions, cancellation/session forfeiture, travel and financing costs. “Monthly” can describe loan repayment rather than treatment frequency. Low advertised prices and same-day pressure can become a much larger contract; current NCAC advice supports taking time to decide.[^ncac-2026]

Pay-as-you-go reduces prepaid exposure but may cost more over repeated visits. A course may lower a per-session quote but adds expiry, capacity, refunds and closure risk; do not forecast solvency or guarantee refunds after closure. Verify advance-payment protection and payment exposure.[^caa-prepay] Verify discount qualifications without collecting unnecessary personal proof in the agent session. Ask who treats complications and whether the cost is included.

## Consultation and visit questions

- Ask about realistic reduction versus complete removal, repeats, treatment spacing and reassessment after poor response.
- Ask about grooming, tanning, products, skin conditions and any medicine/other-procedure precautions; never instruct a medication change.
- Obtain the selected clinic's written shaving/product rules, skin care, bathing/exercise/sun precautions and warning/contact instructions. Hospital-specific shaving fees or old identity-document wording are illustrations, not the current user's instructions.[^hospital-laser]

The MHLW checklist asks the person to understand medicines/devices, risks and alternatives and reconsider rushing.[^mhlw-consent] Qualifying cosmetic medical contracts can have statutory cancellation protections; verify present service, duration, amount, document and timing conditions. Never guarantee every course can be cancelled without cost. The retrieved CAA guide covers specified cosmetic services exceeding **both one month and JPY 50,000**; exactly one month or exactly JPY 50,000 does not meet that threshold. Where applicable, cooling off is generally eight days from the required contract document; verify document sufficiency, counting, exceptions and current law. Do not substitute salon rules or calculate a legal entitlement from these thresholds alone.[^caa-contract][^ncac-contract]
