# Refresh and clinic research

Perform both tracks **at every execution**, even before the concept's stale-after date. Read [research history](research.md) for prior retrieval limits, then [clinical orientation](okf/procedure.md). Do not freeze this bundle into a permanently current protocol.

1. Revalidate the actual cited passages, scope, dates and applicability. Check official publisher hubs for revised documents and safety notices; old patient pages can contain superseded logistics even when clinical orientation remains useful.
2. Rediscover with the Japanese queries below plus the current year and chosen area. Assess official/regulatory sources first, professional guidelines second, university/public hospital education next. A provider's own site is primary for its own price/policy, not independent proof of superior outcomes. Reviews and affiliate rankings are leads only. Verify named clinicians and specialty credentials against relevant professional registers; membership alone is not specialist certification.
3. Search exact branch pages for consultation, treatment, price, terms, consent forms, risks, access, interpreter arrangements and availability. Preserve Japanese labels and translate carefully. Never use a search snippet as a final quote or assume a national chain's offer applies at every branch.
4. Distinguish fact, provider claim, clinician decision, user preference and unknown. Resolve contradictions through current authority and then the clinic/clinician. Clinic contact itself requires future action/data-sharing approval; do not send inquiries during a dry run.
5. Use corrected verified details immediately; if evidence cannot be verified, provide consultation/manual-verification options and explain the limitation. Offer a separate public-source-only correction after helping. Preserve source history; do not invent a verified event from retrieval alone.

Clinic sources and slots must be refreshed before each proposal and immediately before approved submission. Record searched-at time, exact query, inspected candidates, rejected candidates/reasons, failed fetches and supersession. Keep live user-scoped research out of the public repository.

## Shortlist format

Aim for two or three feasible options. Compare the same body area/clinical target, session assumptions and time horizon. If only one fits, explain why; never pad the list. Place a plain-language best fit **for the person's stated priorities** first, with one tradeoff, then let them choose. Do not use an opaque quality score or choose clinical treatment for them.

| Option/branch | Route/procedure | Evidence of fit and clinician checkpoint | Total-cost scenario in JPY | Travel/language/follow-up | Unknowns and reason to choose/avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Populate live | Consultation or clinician-discussed option | Source and checked-at time; no guarantee | Known subtotal + named unknowns; upfront vs financed total | Actual branch support, not English-homepage inference | Explain affordability, quality and suitability separately |

A known subtotal is not a complete total. Mark unpriced required items as unknown, never zero. Separate treatment/consultation charges, anesthesia, tests/medicines, supplies, follow-ups, repeat sessions, transport and likely time away from work. Include taxes for self-pay quotes; distinguish optional charges from required ones. Show cash total, financing fees and total repayment independently of the advertised monthly amount. Avoid contracts whose expiry/available slots make completion infeasible. Ask for written terms before committing and include pay-as-you-go where feasible.

## Japanese questions to prepare, not send

- 総額はいくらですか。追加料金と税込価格を教えてください。 — What is the total, including extra charges and tax?
- 診察で適応とリスク、他の選択肢を説明してもらえますか。 — Can the clinician explain suitability, risks and alternatives?
- 予約変更・キャンセル・解約の条件を教えてください。 — What are rescheduling, cancellation and contract termination terms?
- 英語対応は診察・説明・術後相談でも可能ですか。 — Is English supported for examination, explanation and follow-up?

Give one next action per turn; ask the next material question, with at most five visible items per group. Preserve important uncertainty. No invented duration estimates or medical promises.
